Sector Expertise

M&A expertise, deep in
the industries we know best.

We would rather know a few industries cold than dabble in all of them. When your advisor already knows the buyers, the multiples, and what diligence will surface, you start two steps ahead.

Direct Answer

Salt Creek Advisory runs sell-side and buy-side M&A processes in five sectors: early childhood education and preschools, business services, industrials and manufacturing, MSP and IT services, and dog daycare and pet care. Early childhood education is the flagship practice, because co-founder Connor Pitts worked through more than 40 ECE transactions from inside a national acquirer before starting the firm. What the five have in common is the shape of the business rather than the industry code: founder-owned companies in the $2 million to $75 million revenue range that sell to a small, knowable set of private equity platforms and strategic consolidators. Both principals run every deal personally, and the fee is a success fee at closing with no retainer.

Owner Questions

Picking the right practice area.

The questions owners ask before they decide whether sector experience actually matters for their deal.

Five: early childhood education and preschools, business services, industrials and manufacturing, MSP and IT services, and dog daycare and pet care. Early childhood education is the flagship practice, because co-founder Connor Pitts worked through more than 40 ECE transactions from inside a national acquirer before starting the firm. The other four share the same underlying shape: founder-owned, facility-based or relationship-based businesses that sell to a small, knowable set of private equity platforms and strategic consolidators.
Businesses in roughly the $2 million to $75 million revenue range. That is the lower middle market, and it is deliberately where the firm sits. Below that range the economics rarely support a full competitive process. Above it, larger banks with bigger teams are often the better answer, and we will say so.
Not always, and it is worth being honest about that. Process discipline matters more than sector fluency in some deals, and a strong generalist lower middle market bank can run an excellent process. Sector knowledge matters most in three situations: when the buyer universe is small and specific, when valuation turns on industry metrics a generalist would have to learn on your time, or when real estate sits alongside the operating business. Preschools, MSPs, and dog daycare all meet at least two of those tests. Our guide on how to choose an M&A advisor walks through the rest of the decision.
Ask anyway. The five listed sectors are where we have the deepest buyer relationships, not the only work we take. If your business is a founder-owned company in the lower middle market and we do not think we are the right firm for it, we will tell you that on the first call and point you toward someone who is. Salt Creek Advisory LLC operates as an investment bank in the traditional lower middle market sense: we advise privately held business owners on the sale and acquisition of their companies. We are not a chartered bank or depository institution, we do not accept deposits, and no funds held in connection with an engagement are FDIC or NCUA insured. We are not a broker-dealer registered with FINRA or the SEC and are not a registered investment adviser. To the extent applicable, our M&A engagements are conducted in reliance on the M&A broker exemption at Section 15(b)(13) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (15 U.S.C. § 78o(b)(13)), under which “an M&A broker shall be exempt from registration” when acting solely in connection with the transfer of ownership of an “eligible privately held company,” defined as a company with no registered securities whose EBITDA is less than $25,000,000 or whose gross revenues are less than $250,000,000. That exemption is unavailable to any broker that holds client funds or securities, provides transaction financing, forms or represents a group of buyers, deals with a passive buyer, or represents both sides of a transaction without written disclosure and consent, and we do not engage in any of those activities. We hold or obtain state business broker licensure where required, and where a transaction calls for services only a licensed broker-dealer may perform, those services are referred to and executed through a properly registered broker-dealer. We do not hold, custody, or take possession of client funds or securities at any time. All engagements are advisory in nature; nothing on this site is an offer or solicitation to buy or sell any security, and nothing here is legal, tax, or investment advice.
