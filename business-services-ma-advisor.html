Business Services

Thinking about selling your
business services company?

Business services is the broadest label in M&A, and buyers do not price the label. What they price is whether your revenue renews on its own or has to be won again every year, whether your contracts transfer with the business, and whether the company runs when you are not in it. Most owners have more of that story than they think, and the ones who do not can build it. At Salt Creek Advisory, we help you get it in order, then put enough of the right buyers in the room that they have to compete for it. Both of us on every call, and we get paid when you get paid.

The 2026 Services Market

Buyers do not price the label. They price the contract.

A serious buyer will set your industry code aside and read your agreements: what renews by default, what gets re-bid, what transfers without a customer’s consent, and what one wage increase does to the margin. There is no business services multiple.
The number follows whether your revenue renews by default.

Rollins tells its shareholders in its 2025 Form 10-K that roughly 75% of its business is recurring service under scheduled agreements. Robert Half tells its shareholders that clients can terminate on short notice and without penalty. Both sit in the same business services category, and they do not sell for the same price. The ranges below reflect that. What moves you up the range is evidence: agreements that auto-renew with a volume floor, retention you can show from the invoices, contracts that transfer without a consent campaign, and a management team that runs the business without you.

What Earns a Premium

Premiums are earned
in the contract file.

These are the six things buyers dig into once they get past your revenue number. We help you get each one ready before we ever go to market, which is also most of what buyers look for in any acquisition target.

01

Revenue That Renews by Default

Four questions settle it: does it auto-renew, is there a volume floor, what does exit cost the customer, and who has to act for the revenue to continue. If the answer to the last one is nobody, it is recurring. If it is your salesperson, it is a pipeline with a contract stapled to it.

02

Retention You Can Prove

Net revenue retention by cohort, from invoices that carry a stable customer ID. Without it, a buyer rebuilds the number from three years of extracts, treats every unmatched account as churn, and calls twenty to thirty of your customers.

03

Contracts That Transfer

Anti-assignment and change-of-control clauses turn contracted revenue into a consent campaign. It is one of the more common reasons a services deal gets repriced after the letter of intent, and adding permitted-assignment language at renewal costs nothing.

04

Low Concentration

Highland Global’s illustrative ladder starts discounting at 25% to 35% in one customer. Your lender is stricter and arrives earlier: a buyer who wants you at 7x but can finance only 5.5x restructures with a larger seller note or earnout.

05

Pass-Through Labor

Direct labor was 68% of ABM’s revenue in fiscal 2025. On cost-plus contracts a wage increase passes through; on fixed-price contracts it lands on you until the re-bid. Buyers ask what share of your revenue sits on each.

06

Management Depth

A team that runs the business without you, and reporting a sponsor can consolidate without rebuilding. Those are two of the four tests that separate platform pricing from add-on pricing, and both are knowable in advance.

How We Would Run Your Sale

What we actually do
for a business services owner.

01

Read the Contract Book First

We sort every agreement by the four questions before a buyer does: auto-renewal, volume floor, exit cost, and who has to act. Then we tag revenue as recurring or re-won at the account level, so the number the multiple gets applied to is one you chose, not one a buyer reconstructed.

02

Rebuild Retention From the Invoices

Customer-level revenue by year, matched on account ID, with net revenue retention by cohort and concentration by customer. We flag assignment and change-of-control language early, so consents are a plan rather than a surprise between the letter of intent and closing.

03

Decide Platform or Add-On, on Purpose

We position you against the test a sponsor actually runs: a team that runs without you, reporting that consolidates, a market with targets left, and systems a tuck-in can be loaded onto. Then we compare offers on cash at close, not the headline multiple.

04

Run the Competition

Every relevant platform at the table at the same time, with the same information. Our target is first offers within 30 days. Competition is what turns a fair number into a real one.

Who Is Buying

There are plenty of buyers. No two value you the same way.

Permanent-capital holders, platform sponsors, and the platforms they already own can price the same services company very differently. Fit, integration model, rollover, and your role after close can outweigh the headline. It helps to know how strategic buyers and private equity buyers think differently before you pick one.
